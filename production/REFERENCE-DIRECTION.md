## What takes precedence

1. **All newly authored type is sans-serif.** Use the corrected Inter Tight variable font. Earlier serif, italic, script and mixed-font directions are historical reference descriptions, not current instructions. The original organic Manwill wordmark is supplied artwork and remains unchanged.
2. **Headline words have equal treatment.** Do not enlarge one word, alternate sizes within a headline, add an italic last word, or use mixed weights to manufacture variety. Website h1/h2 headings share one size and weight per breakpoint. Artwork variety must come from composition, crop, placement, photographic character and the relationship between type and image. Equal type does not mean repeating the same upper-left text block and corner logo on every image.
3. **Keep the photography large.** Show complete final artworks at their native ratios. Do not hide the work in disclosures, reduce it to small gallery cards, or crop completed posters with `object-fit: cover`.
4. **Hide faces**, including partial faces in reflections. Use hands, knees, backs, shoulders, clothes, stools and gestures. Keep anatomy, objects and coffee preparation plausible.
5. **Preserve original marks.** Use `assets/brand/manwill-hand-lettered-v2.svg` with its exact paths and proportions. Do not redraw it, approximate it with a font, stretch it or ask an image model to invent a replacement. Agent-designed alternatives under `assets/identity/` are superseded. The selected shirt photograph is an accepted physical mockup; preserve it rather than repeatedly regenerating its print.
6. **The accepted argument stays visible.** A personal encounter with the brewer leads. Familiar preferences and visible making support that same relationship. The wordmark, colours, photographs and applications need to make those ideas concrete.
7. **Do not claim concept photographs depict Manwill's actual people or premises.** The actual shop photographs are separately sourced evidence. Their signage is documentary and must remain unaltered, even where the existing sign contains serif lettering.

The website currently uses 48px headings on desktop, 42px on tablet and 36px on mobile, all at weight 550; running copy is 18px desktop/tablet and 16px mobile. Functional text is 14px. These accepted website tokens are not a reason to flatten every image into the same composition.

## The argument the images must support

- **Lead: an encounter with the brewer.** Manwill's compact counter provides the observed starting point for exploring direct conversation. The informal original mark, people photography and shirt invitation express that possibility. This is a proposed direction, not a proved explanation of customer loyalty.
- **Support: discovery through familiar preferences.** “Tell us what you drink at home.” belongs on the menu/card as a useful opening question. It gives the discovery work a purpose beyond another coffee poster.
- **Support: pleasure in watching the making.** Pouring, filter preparation and the intended coffee-written-mark transition connect the identity to gestures of preparation. Vary the action, framing and light rather than showing the same equipment repeatedly.

Research context remains Coffee Ideas in Auroville, Subko's Bloom Bar and Blue Tokai's manual-brewing workshops. These references support the discussion; they do not prove a unique market gap. Keep the accepted visible rationale and Raagul's concrete offer: positioning, identity/language, photography direction, menus, space and digital communication. Do not turn the proposal into a criticism of the existing brand.

Approved campaign language is “Meet your brewer.”, “Tell us what you drink at home.” and “A front-row seat to your coffee.” The shirt uses “ASK ME ABOUT YOUR COFFEE.” The menu's support line is “We’ll help you choose.” The existing making artwork also uses “WATCH YOUR COFFEE BEING MADE.” Do not revive “Make yourself a regular”, “Coffee, by hand” or “Brewed by hand”, copy reference slogans, or add generic slowness/wellness claims.
